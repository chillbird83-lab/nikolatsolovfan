Race Analysis · Round 12

Baku: from 11th to 2nd, and seven points to find.

By Nikolay Tomov · Published 26 September 2026 · 7 min read

Nikola Tsolov left Baku without the championship lead he had held since Silverstone. He also left it having driven what the Bulgarian broadcast called one of the best races of his year. Both things are true, and the second one matters more for how the last two rounds will go.

Baku ran three races in two days: a Sprint and Feature Race 1 on Friday, Feature Race 2 on Saturday. Friday went badly. Tsolov faded from reverse-grid pole to fifth in the Sprint, then retired from Feature 1 and picked up a grid penalty on the way out. On Saturday he started 11th, finished second with the fastest lap, and was closing on the winner at the flag. Rafael Câmara inherited that win and leads the championship 207 to 200.

Friday: a Sprint that faded and a Feature that ended early

Reverse-grid pole put Tsolov at the front of the Sprint, but the Campos car had little to defend with. Dino Beganovic took the lead on lap 6, the rear tyres were, in Tsolov's words on the radio, gone, and an oversteer moment into Turn 1 on lap 10 cost him second place to Laurens van Hoepen. He finished fifth for four points. Beganovic won his second race in a row. Câmara scored nothing: a 10-second penalty for hitting Mari Boya dropped him to 18th.

Feature 1 was worse. Tsolov started eighth, pitted early and was caught up in a multi-car incident at Turn 3, with Joshua Dürksen, Cian Shields, John Bennett and Rafael Villagómez also involved, that brought out the red flag. His race ended there. Alexander Dunne won from pole for his first victory of the season, leading a Rodin one-two ahead of Martinius Stenshorne, and Câmara took fourth by passing Noel León on the final straight of the final lap. Those twelve points made him championship leader by a single point, 182 to 181.

The stewards then added a second cost. On his way into the pits in Feature 1, Tsolov had crossed the white line at the pit entry by about a metre with his right-hand wheels. That carries a five-second penalty, and because he never finished the race to serve it, it became a three-place grid drop. He had qualified eighth for Saturday. He would start 11th.

Saturday: 11th to 2nd

Everyone started Feature 2 on the supersoft tyre except Shields, so there was no strategy to hide behind. Tsolov gained places on the opening lap around the outside of Dürksen and was fighting Ritomo Miyata and Beganovic for the edge of the top ten when the first Safety Car came out for Bennett, who had been spun at Turn 1.

The race turned on the neutralisations. Dunne pitted just before a Virtual Safety Car on lap 7, when Miyata stopped on track. Tsolov and Câmara stayed out. When a second VSC for Tasanapol Inthraphuvasak ended, both came in on the same lap. Invicta's stop was slow, and Tsolov left the pit lane just behind the leading group instead of stuck in the middle of the field. Before the stops he had been more than half a minute behind Câmara on the road. After them he was a few seconds away.

From there it was pace. He passed van Hoepen with DRS into Turn 1, set the fastest lap of the race at 1:55.715, and took Stenshorne's gap from over three seconds to nothing in a handful of laps. Stenshorne had one of the quickest cars down the 2.2-kilometre straight and Tsolov one of the slowest, so the move had to be built in the first two sectors. He made one look on the straight, backed out rather than risk both cars at Turn 1, and completed it cleanly a lap later. That was the podium.

Up front, Dunne had been told of his own five-second penalty, for the same pit-entry line offence Tsolov committed on Friday. Câmara did not need to pass him. Second on the road would be the win, so he sat behind Dunne and used his DRS to stay clear of the car coming up behind him. Tsolov was under a second back on the final lap. Dunne crossed the line first, dropped to third with the penalty, and Câmara won.

I saw also Nikola was looking very strong behind me, so I just tried to stay as close as I can to Alex to save the maximum points. Rafael Câmara · podium interview, 26 September 2026

Tsolov's own verdict came over the radio: "We didn't give up. It's huge. Practically the slowest, we became the fastest today." In the press conference he put it more quietly. "Overall I am pleased with the result, but I am more pleased with what we did, to be honest."

Tsolov went into the summer break 20 points clear. Since then Câmara has scored 62 points to his 33, and the lead has changed hands. It is also now a three-way fight. Dunne's 50-point weekend put him 18 behind Câmara and 11 behind Tsolov, and on Saturday the top three in the standings were the top three on the podium. Minì, 44 back, is effectively out of it.

What it means with two to go

The Honest Verdict

The lead is gone, but so is the slide.

Friday cost Tsolov the championship lead, and part of that was his own doing: the pit-entry line cost him three grid places he could not afford. Saturday showed the other side of him. From 11th, in a car short on straight-line speed, he was the fastest driver on the circuit and finished close enough behind Câmara to make the Invicta driver defend.

Seven points is less than one good Feature Race. Tsolov still has six wins, the most on the grid, and Feature 2 was his first podium since Spa. Two rounds remain, Lusail on 26–28 November and Yas Island on 4–6 December. He goes into both as the chaser for the first time since June.

Frequently asked questions

Who won the F2 Baku 2026 races?

Baku ran three races. Dino Beganovic (DAMS) won the Sprint, Alexander Dunne (Rodin) won Feature Race 1 from pole, and Rafael Câmara (Invicta) won Feature Race 2 after Dunne, who crossed the line first, was given a five-second penalty for crossing the pit-entry line.

Where did Nikola Tsolov finish at Baku?

Fifth in the Sprint after starting from reverse-grid pole, a retirement in Feature Race 1 after a multi-car incident at Turn 3, and second in Feature Race 2 from 11th on the grid, with the fastest lap. He scored 23 points across the weekend.

Why did Tsolov start Feature Race 2 from 11th?

He qualified eighth but took a three-place grid drop. In Feature Race 1 he crossed the white line at the pit entry, which carries a five-second penalty; because he retired from that race, the stewards converted it into a grid penalty for the next one.

What is the F2 championship situation after Baku?

Rafael Câmara leads on 207 points, Tsolov is second on 200 and Alexander Dunne third on 189. Gabriele Minì is fourth on 163. Two rounds remain: Lusail (26–28 November) and Yas Island (4–6 December).
