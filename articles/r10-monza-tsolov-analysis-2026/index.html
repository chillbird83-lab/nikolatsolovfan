Monza: a careful weekend, and the gap comes down to eleven.

By Nikolay Tomov · Published 6 September 2026 · 6 min read

Some weekends you win the title fight, and some you just get through it. Monza was the second kind for Nikola Tsolov. He started the Feature ninth after a grid penalty, spent the afternoon in the tight midfield, and came home seventh. Add Saturday's fifth in the Sprint and it was ten points banked from a weekend that never really came to him. The championship lead survives, but it is smaller: 11 points, and the name behind it has changed.

The weekend belonged to Joshua Dürksen, who swept both races for Invicta. And it belonged, in the title picture, to his teammate Rafael Câmara, whose second place on Sunday lifted him past Gabriele Minì into second in the championship. Minì, at his home race, left with nothing.

The shape of the title fight changed in a weekend. Câmara has out-scored everyone across Silverstone, Spa and now Monza, and his Invicta is the fastest car on a power circuit. Minì's blank at Monza, on home ground, drops him to 30 back and effectively out of the leading two. So the run to the finish is Tsolov and Câmara, 11 points apart, with four rounds left and Dürksen the wildcard now up to fourth on the win count.

Tsolov's weekend: manage it, bank it

This was a race-by-race weekend by design. With a comfortable lead and a car that qualified out of position, the plan was simple: avoid the first-chicane chaos, look after the supersoft tyre, and take whatever the day offered. Tsolov's camp said as much before the start, framing Monza as a points weekend rather than a win-or-nothing gamble.

From ninth he made the start count, as he usually does, and settled into a long midfield scrap around Ritomo Miyata, Tasanapol Inthraphuvasak and Martinius Stenshorne. On a track where the slipstream giveth and taketh away, those places changed hands lap after lap. He cleared Stenshorne and got the better of the fading Inthraphuvasak, one of the harder passes of the day into the Roggia. Late on there was light contact with Stenshorne exiting the second chicane that cost the Norwegian a front-wing endplate, and in the closing laps Alex Dunne had just enough to keep Tsolov behind for sixth. Seventh it was, and no drama attached to it.

The front: an all-Invicta duel, and a sweep

Câmara took pole and lined up alongside Dürksen, and the two Invicta cars spent the first stint trading the lead at the first chicane, swapping places with DRS more than once and with no team orders to settle it. Dürksen pitted early to undercut; Câmara stayed out longer and led on track until the stops cycled through. A safety car for Colton Herta's crash at Parabolica bunched the field with half the race to run, and Dürksen nailed the restart. Câmara chased him down to three tenths at Parabolica on the last lap but never found a way by.

It was Dürksen's fourth win of 2026 and his eighth in F2, level with Charles Leclerc and George Russell on the all-time list, in his third season in the category. Câmara took the second place that mattered most for his championship. León completed the podium with another strong drive, ahead of Bennett and Miyata.

What it means with four to go

The Honest Verdict

Damage limited, but the cushion is thinner.

Ninth on the grid at a track where his rivals had the quicker car, Tsolov did the sensible thing and drove home a clean seventh, with fifth in the Sprint on top. That is exactly how a leader is supposed to handle a weekend that is not his. The lead is still his, and he has now scored in every round bar one all season.

The worry is the trend. Câmara and Invicta have the edge on high-speed circuits, and Baku, later in this run, is another. Eleven points is a cushion, not a comfort, and the margin for a bad afternoon is shrinking. Next up is the brand-new Madring in Madrid on 11–13 September, a track nobody has raced, where everyone starts level.

Frequently asked questions

Who won the F2 Monza 2026 Feature Race?

Joshua Dürksen (Invicta) won the Feature after also winning the Sprint, a clean sweep of Monza. It was his fourth win of 2026 and his eighth in F2, equalling the record for career F2 wins. Câmara was second, León third.

Where did Nikola Tsolov finish at Monza?

Fifth in the Sprint and seventh in the Feature, the latter from ninth on the grid after a post-qualifying penalty. A quiet, clean weekend that kept him top of the championship.

What is the F2 championship situation after Monza?

Tsolov leads on 177, Câmara up to second on 166 (11 behind), Minì third on 147 after a scoreless home weekend, Dunne fourth on 118. Four rounds remain, starting with Madrid.

Who is Tsolov's closest title rival now?

Rafael Câmara. His Monza second place moved him past Minì into P2, 11 points back, and his Invicta has been the strongest car on fast tracks. He is the main threat over the final four rounds.
