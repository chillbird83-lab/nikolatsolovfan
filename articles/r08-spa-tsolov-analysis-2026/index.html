Race Analysis · Round 8

Third through the chaos: a gravel trap, a red flag, and twenty-seven points.

By Nikolay Tomov · Published 19 July 2026 · 7 min read

Some weekends the championship is won with trophies. Others it is won with damage control. Spa-Francorchamps was the second kind: a Feature Race with safety cars, a red flag, a restart against a countdown clock instead of a lap count, penalties flying in every direction, and rain arriving over the pit lane on the final lap. Nikola Tsolov ran through the gravel at La Source, dropped to twelfth when his supersofts collapsed, picked his way back, and was classified third. The championship lead, seventeen points on Friday morning, is twenty-seven at the halfway mark of the season.

Rafael Câmara won it — pole, victory, fastest lap, his second Feature win of the year, and Invicta's fourth straight Spa victory across two seasons after Joshua Dürksen's Saturday Sprint. Tasanapol Inthraphuvasak took his maiden F2 podium in second. But the driver who left Belgium in the best championship position was the one who finished third.

The top of the table did not reshuffle — it stretched. Tsolov out-scored Minì 20 to 10 across the weekend, the fifth round in a row he has taken more points than the Italian. Câmara's 31-point weekend makes him the form driver of July, and he still needed a perfect Sunday just to close to 36 behind. Tsolov has now scored in ten consecutive races and stood on the podium in every round since Monaco.

Saturday: seventh to fourth, and a rival's retirement

Qualifying P4 — 0.393 off Câmara's pole in a session where pole to tenth was covered by under six tenths — put Tsolov seventh on the Sprint reverse grid. He worked forward through a race twice interrupted by the safety car and took fourth on the final lap, around Sebastian Montoya into La Source. Five points, no risks taken.

The bigger Saturday news happened in the pit lane: Gabriele Minì retired the MP Motorsport car. Zero points, and the pattern since Silverstone has become the story of his July.

Sunday, part one: the start, the gravel, and the collapse

The Feature began on a cool track — 14°C air, rain flirting with the startline — with the front four all gambling on the supersoft. Tsolov's launch from P4 was another strong one: he slotted into third as Inthraphuvasak beat Câmara off the line, while behind them Minì clattered into Bilinski at La Source (a five-second penalty followed), Beganovic moved before the lights and stalled, and Goethe stopped on the exit of Turn 1. Safety car.

The restart brought the moment of the race. Tsolov drew alongside Câmara on the run to La Source and was ahead at the apex from the outside line — then Câmara held his line on the exit and left no room. Tsolov went through the gravel, dropped behind Dunne and Dürksen, and damaged a front-wing endplate. Race control looked at it and took no action. The team radio was blunt:

What happened with Câmara? Is there no further action? I was fully in front, and he didn't leave space. — Nikola Tsolov, team radio

Then the supersoft did what half the paddock feared it would. Around Lap 6 the front-runners' tyres let go almost together, and everyone who had started on them — Câmara, Inthraphuvasak, Dunne, Tsolov — dived in for mediums, dropping behind the alternative-strategy runners. Tsolov fell as far as twelfth. Moments later Laurens van Hoepen lost the car over an exit kerb at high speed and speared into the barriers — a huge accident he walked away from — and after the safety car came the red flag, with barrier repairs and spilled fluids to clear.

Sunday, part two: the clock, the shuffle, and the podium

The stoppage reset the race in Tsolov's favour in two ways: Campos replaced his damaged nose for free, and the restart came with a countdown clock — around 21 minutes — instead of the remaining laps. The runners ahead of him still owed their mandatory pit stops; his was already done.

From there it was patience and traffic. He cleared Shields, latched onto Inthraphuvasak, and when the medium-tyre leaders finally made their stops under a late safety car — triggered by contact between Dunne and Fittipaldi — Tsolov cycled up to fifth with 14 minutes on the clock. On the last restart he took Inthraphuvasak into Turn 1, then lost the place back when the Thai driver, with stronger tyres, re-passed before Les Combes. The final minutes were about defence — Minì, recovered from last to right behind him, never got close enough — and the track-limits stewards did the rest: five-second penalties for Dunne and León lifted Tsolov to third in the classification.

It was that kind of afternoon: Tsolov himself finished it under a black-and-white flag for track limits, two strikes deep. Câmara crossed the line just before the rain reached the pit lane.

The post-race twist: León disqualified

Hours later the stewards excluded Noel León from the results entirely — not for anything on track, but because a team guest in the Campos pit area wore a headset with a live microphone during the red-flag period, equipment the rules reserve for operational personnel. The team also had 25 teams'-championship points withdrawn, suspended pending no further violations. Harsh on León, who had driven from the back of the grid into the points and lost his second result of the weekend to procedure — his Friday qualifying laps had already been deleted for reversing in the pit lane.

What it means at halfway

The Honest Verdict

The bad weekends are now this good.

Seven rounds ago a race like this — gravel, damage, a strategy that fell apart through no fault of his own — would have been a zero. At Spa it produced twenty points and a bigger lead, because Tsolov kept the car intact, took the positions that were available, and let his rivals hand back the rest. At the halfway mark the numbers read: 161 points, six wins, ten straight scores, a podium in five consecutive rounds.

The caution: Câmara has out-scored everyone across Silverstone and Spa, and Invicta is the strongest team on Sunday pace right now. Minì is wounded, but 27 points is three good races. Budapest comes in five days — a track where overtaking is scarce and qualifying, still Tsolov's least strong discipline, matters most.

Frequently asked questions

Who won the F2 Spa-Francorchamps 2026 Feature Race?

Rafael Câmara (Invicta Racing) won from pole with the fastest lap — his second Feature win of the season and Invicta's fourth straight Spa victory across two years. Tasanapol Inthraphuvasak (ART) took his maiden F2 podium in second, with Nikola Tsolov (Campos) classified third at +7.463 after five-second track-limits penalties for Alexander Dunne and Noel León.

Why was the race red-flagged?

Laurens van Hoepen (Trident) lost the car over an exit kerb in the middle sector and hit the barriers hard — he was unhurt. With barrier repairs and spilled fluids to clear, the race was suspended on Lap 10 of 25 and restarted against a countdown clock rather than the full distance.

Why was Noel León disqualified afterwards?

A team guest in the Campos pit area used a headset with a live microphone during the red-flag period — equipment restricted to operational personnel. The stewards excluded León's car from the results and withdrew 25 of Campos Racing's teams' points, suspended pending no further violations.

What is the F2 championship situation after Spa 2026?

Tsolov leads on 161 from Minì on 134 (gap 27) and Câmara on 125, with Dunne fourth on 108. Six rounds remain, starting with Budapest on 24–26 July.
