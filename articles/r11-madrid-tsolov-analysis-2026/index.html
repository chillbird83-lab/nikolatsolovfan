Race Analysis · Round 11

Madrid: a weekend to forget, and the lead down to seven.

By Nikolay Tomov · Published 13 September 2026 · 6 min read

The championship has looked, for months, like a thing Nikola Tsolov could manage rather than one that could get away from him. Madrid was the reminder that it can still bite. He crashed out of Saturday's Sprint while running fifth and clearly the quicker car, then gambled on the hard tyre in Sunday's Feature and finished eighteenth, out of the points. Two races, no score. The lead is still his, but it is now seven points, not eleven, and Rafael Câmara is right there.

It happened at a track nobody in Formula 2 had raced before. The new Madring, a street circuit on the edge of the city, was tight, physical and almost impossible to overtake on, closer in character to Monaco than to anything the field had seen since June. Both Max Verstappen and Lando Norris, racing the Formula 1 event alongside, praised the atmosphere and criticised the layout for the same reason: it is a wonderful thing to drive and a hard thing to race. That combination is exactly what caught Tsolov out.

Saturday: quick, then into the wall

The Sprint should have been a points haul. Tsolov had genuine pace, around a second a lap quicker than the cars around him by his own reckoning, but he was stuck behind Campos teammate Noel León, who defended hard even as he waved the faster Trident of Laurens van Hoepen through. Tsolov went to the radio asking to be let by, tried a move into Turn 5, ran off track to complete it, and gave the place back. A few corners later it ended. A brush with the wall at Turn 20, a slight touch on the rear-left, and the car snapped into a slow spin that brought out the Safety Car. Day over from fifth.

His own account afterward was strikingly calm. He described stopping a metre or two too early into Turn 20, the car turning a fraction more than usual, and a "mega minimal" contact that a heavier F2 car simply cannot recover from once it unsettles.

Today was one of those difficult days, a small mistake with big consequences. The pace was brilliant. I felt exactly like at Silverstone, where I had far more potential. I believe this day happened for a reason and it will make me much stronger. I'm looking at tomorrow with full confidence. Nikola Tsolov · post-race, 12 September 2026

Out front, Ritomo Miyata drove a composed race to a maiden F2 win, managing two Safety Car restarts to beat Emerson Fittipaldi and van Hoepen. It was Hitech's first victory since the 2025 Baku Sprint.

Sunday: the gamble that a Safety Car killed

Starting fifth, Tsolov was the lead runner on the alternate strategy, the only front car to start on the hard tyre while the leaders ran softs. The logic was the one that won him Barcelona: run long, keep the tyre alive, and cash in when everyone else has to stop. The early laps were treacherous on cold hards, and he nearly lost the car through La Monumental before catching a big slide, one of the saves of the afternoon.

Then the plan came apart. Kush Maini crashed into the wall at Turn 18 barely into the race, and the Safety Car that followed handed the soft-tyre runners cheap laps and shrank the hard tyre's whole advantage. On a track where losing track position means losing it for good, the long stint never got its payback. Late on Tsolov lost tenth to Roman Bilinski, then Mari Boya, León, van Hoepen and Sebastián Montoya all cleared him on the penultimate lap. He took the flag eighteenth, outside the points.

Dino Beganovic made the whole thing look easy from the front. He converted pole into a lights-to-flag win by more than seven seconds, his first of the season and a bounce-back from a difficult Monza for DAMS. Alexander Dunne took second, Miyata a second podium of the weekend in third. Câmara crossed the line higher but a 10-second penalty for tipping Oliver Goethe into the wall dropped him to tenth. Even so, sixth in the Sprint and that tenth in the Feature meant he scored on both days, which was all his championship needed.

Câmara has now clawed more than twenty points off the deficit he carried out of the summer break, most of it through Tsolov's misfortune and his own consistency, and the gap is down to seven with three rounds left. Minì's third place is no longer safe either, with Dunne closing to fourteen behind him after a strong run of results. The title, though, is a two-car fight again, and Câmara's Invicta has the momentum.

What it means with three to go

The Honest Verdict

The first genuinely bad weekend, at the worst time.

This was not a slow weekend, it was a costly one. The Sprint pace was there and the Feature strategy was a fair call that a Safety Car undid. But a leader is measured on the bad days as much as the good, and Madrid was the first round all year where Tsolov left with nothing while his nearest rival scored twice. The maths still favours him, and the way he spoke afterward suggests the head is in the right place.

The concern is that the buffer is thin now and Baku is next, another street circuit where Invicta and Câmara should be quick. Seven points can vanish in a single afternoon. The good news, if there is any, is that the run to the title now demands the version of Tsolov that turns disappointment into results. He has done it all season. He needs it again from 24 September.

Frequently asked questions

Who won the F2 Madrid 2026 Feature Race?

Dino Beganovic (DAMS) won from pole, leading every lap to take the flag by over seven seconds. It was his first win of 2026. Alexander Dunne was second, Ritomo Miyata third.

Who won the F2 Madrid 2026 Sprint Race?

Ritomo Miyata (Hitech) took his maiden F2 victory, managing two Safety Car restarts to beat Emerson Fittipaldi and Laurens van Hoepen.

Where did Nikola Tsolov finish at Madrid?

He crashed out of the Sprint while running fifth after brushing the wall at Turn 20, then finished eighteenth in the Feature from fifth on the grid, out of the points, when the alternate hard-tyre strategy failed to pay off. No points across the weekend.

What is the F2 championship situation after Madrid?

Tsolov still leads on 177, Câmara up to 170 and just 7 behind, Minì third on 153, Dunne fourth on 139. Three rounds remain, starting at Baku on 24–26 September.
