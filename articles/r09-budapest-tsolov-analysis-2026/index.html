Race Analysis · Round 9

Twelfth to seventh: turning a bad Saturday into damage control.

By Nikolay Tomov · Published 26 July 2026 · 7 min read

The Hungaroring is where overtaking goes to die. On a hot, dry Sunday it is Monte Carlo without the walls: track position is everything, and a driver starting twelfth usually finishes about there. Nikola Tsolov started twelfth, carrying a grid penalty from Saturday's sprint, and finished seventh. On this circuit, on this day, that is a good afternoon's work — and with his title rivals scattered behind him, it kept the championship lead at twenty points going into the summer break.

The win went to the other Campos car. Noel León drove a near-perfect race and timed his pit stop to the second, taking his first Feature victory and third F2 win of the year. Kush Maini was second with the fastest lap; Rafael Câmara, who started on the front row, could only manage third. For Tsolov, the story was simpler: score what the day allows, and lose as little ground as possible.

This was the round Tsolov's rivals had circled: he was starting deep, and every point they took was a point off the lead. They barely dented it. Minì won the sprint but finished the Feature ninth, so across the weekend he took thirteen points to Tsolov's six. Câmara's third place clawed back nine of the thirty-one points he trailed by. The gap to Minì is twenty, to Câmara twenty-two, with five rounds left. A weekend built to hurt the leader cost him almost nothing.

The cost of Saturday

The grid penalty was self-inflicted, and Tsolov said so. He qualified seventh on Friday — a scrappy session for Campos, the car nervous on entry — then ran third for most of Saturday's sprint before a late lunge at Sebastián Montoya into Turn 1 ended in contact and a retirement for both. The stewards ruled him predominantly to blame; the ten-second penalty, unservable after a DNF, became a five-place grid drop for the Feature.

Maybe the mistake was taking an unnecessary risk, more than the overtake itself. I probably just needed to wait a few more laps. — Nikola Tsolov, on the sprint incident

It was the same late, committed move that beat Montoya cleanly at Spa a week earlier. This time the room was not there. To his credit, the reflection was immediate, and the racing on Sunday showed no sign of a driver who had lost his nerve.

The start, and the clean line

From twelfth, on the dirty inside of the grid, Tsolov did the sensible thing: he took the long, clean outside line into Turn 1, kept out of the first-corner scrum, and was already picking up places by Turn 4. It was the opposite of Saturday — patient where the sprint had been rash — and it set up everything that followed. Nico Varrone had gained six places off the line to sit ahead of him; Tsolov cleared him early, then set about the cars in front on merit.

The temperature helped. The track climbed past 45°C, and Campos's car has been the class of the field in the heat all season. The drivers who started on the soft tyre — most of the top ten — began to slide backwards as their rubber gave up. Tsolov, on the harder medium and the alternative strategy, was soon the fastest man on the circuit, quicker than the leaders while running in clean air.

The move of the day

He passed Montoya again, this time with room to spare and no drama, then reeled in and cleared Gabriele Minì. The one that will be replayed came late, against Joshua Dürksen at Turn 5 — a corner where passes essentially never happen, taken from the outside line on the dustier part of the track. Only two drivers pulled off a move there all day, and both drove for Campos.

Dino Beganovic, on the same tyre and a similar strategy, was the one who got away: Tsolov closed to within striking distance in the final laps but ran out of circuit. Seventh, from twelfth, on a track where the raw speed to move forward is often not enough — the execution was the point.

León's day, and Campos's day

The headline result belonged to Tsolov's teammate. Noel León started deep in the top ten, ran a disciplined stint, and pitted a lap before a virtual safety car — a small window of luck on top of a strong drive that vaulted him ahead of Maini and Câmara. He crossed the line in tears on the radio. It is the season León's talent has deserved but rarely delivered, after a run of bad luck that included a disqualification at Spa.

For the team it was a statement Sunday: a win and a hard-charging recovery from the sister car, and confirmation that Campos own the best race pace in the field even when qualifying lets them down. On current form it is difficult to see anyone catching them in the teams' championship.

What it means into the break

The Honest Verdict

The lead survived the weekend it was supposed to lose.

This was the hardest hand Tsolov has been dealt all year: a self-made grid penalty at a track where you cannot pass, with three rivals lined up ahead of him and the summer break to stew on any damage. He answered it with a P12-to-P7 drive, and the numbers held — 167 points, a 20-point lead, five rounds to go. Minì and Câmara needed a big weekend at his expense and got a small one.

The caution is the same one Saturday wrote: the qualifying deficit is real, and starting deep on tracks like this is a lottery he will not always win. But the race pace is a genuine weapon, and a bad weekend that ends with the lead intact is exactly what a title campaign is supposed to look like. Monza is next, on 4–6 September.

Frequently asked questions

Where did Tsolov finish in the F2 Budapest 2026 Feature Race?

Seventh, from twelfth on the grid. He served a five-place penalty carried over from the sprint, then gained five places on merit — including a rare pass around the outside of Turn 5 — for six points, keeping the championship lead.

Who won the F2 Budapest 2026 Feature Race?

Noel León (Campos) took his first Feature win and third F2 victory of 2026, helped by pitting just before a virtual safety car. Kush Maini (ART) was second with the fastest lap and Rafael Câmara (Invicta) third.

Why did Tsolov start the Feature from twelfth?

He qualified seventh, but a five-place grid penalty for causing the sprint collision with Sebastián Montoya dropped him to twelfth. The stewards ruled Car 6 predominantly to blame; because he retired from the sprint, the ten-second time penalty converted to a grid drop for the Feature.

What is the F2 championship situation after Budapest 2026?

Tsolov leads on 167 from Minì on 147 (gap 20) and Câmara on 145, with Dunne fourth on 108. Nine of fourteen rounds are done; Round 10 is Monza on 4–6 September, after the summer break.
