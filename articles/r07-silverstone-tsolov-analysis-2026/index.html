Race Analysis · Round 7

The Silverstone double: a sweep, a record, and seventeen points.

By Nikolay Tomov · Published 5 July 2026 · 8 min read

Nikola Tsolov arrived at Silverstone expecting one of the hardest weekends of his season. Campos had been open about it internally, and so had he. He left with both trophies, a hat-trick of wins going back to the Spielberg Feature, and a championship lead of 17 points. His own team radio after the flag put it best.

We expected a difficult weekend, and it was difficult — for our rivals. — Nikola Tsolov, team radio after the Feature Race

Winning the Sprint and the Feature in the same weekend had not been done in Formula 2 since Zane Maloney swept Bahrain for Rodin at the start of 2024. Tsolov did it at Silverstone, from fifth on the qualifying sheet, without ever starting from the front row.

The weekend swing was brutal for Minì: 35 points for Tsolov against 16 for the Italian, who went from leading the championship on Friday night to trailing by 17 on Sunday. Tsolov has now scored in eight consecutive races since the Montréal penalty wiped out his Canadian weekend, and six of his seven rounds this season have produced at least one win.

Saturday: the Sprint that took the lead

Qualifying on Friday was closer than the P5 suggests — Câmara's pole lap was a 1:39.690 and Tsolov was 0.282 off it, with five drivers inside three tenths. Under the reverse-grid rule that put Tsolov sixth for Saturday's Sprint, and he came through to win it by 1.291 seconds over Minì, with Villagómez third. His second Sprint win of the season after Miami, and the result that first put him at the top of the standings — level with Minì on 116 points, ahead on wins.

The Sprint mattered for a second reason: it told Campos the race pace was there. The talk of a difficult weekend had been built on qualifying projections, not race running. By Saturday evening the question had flipped from whether Tsolov could defend his position in the title fight to whether anyone could stop the sweep.

Sunday: the start that won it

Tsolov started the 29-lap Feature from fifth on soft tyres, in cooler conditions than the rest of the weekend. The start decided everything. A gap opened between the two columns of cars into Turn 1 and Tsolov drove straight through it — from P5 to P2 in the length of the run to the first corner, tucked in behind Kush Maini, who had made the other great start of the race. Pole-sitter Câmara lost the lead, ran wide in the opening exchanges, and spent the afternoon in the pack; Invicta's pit wall spent the first laps debating with itself over the radio whether he should hand a place back to Dunne.

The soft tyre had a short life on Silverstone's loading, and the front-runners on it — Maini, Tsolov, Câmara, Dunne — all pitted early, on Lap 6, for the hard. Tsolov rejoined just behind Maini and settled into the long middle stint: 23 laps on one set of hards, with the alternative-strategy runners — Villagómez, Dürksen, Minì among them — running long up ahead on the track but far behind on race maths.

Maggotts–Becketts, around the outside

From Lap 19 Tsolov closed on Maini and began working on him. Two attempts around the outside of Turn 9 came to nothing — Maini defended hard but fair, and gave him racing room. Then came the move of the season so far: around the outside on the entry to Maggotts–Becketts, the fastest sequence of corners on the calendar, where there is one line and no margin. The Bulgarian commentary called it an overtake you see once in ten years at that piece of road. Maini's own verdict, caught later in the drivers' room, was simpler: "Your starts are crazy... that was smart."

The timing was the point, not the spectacle. Maini's pace was fading and every lap spent behind him was a lap gifted to Villagómez and the long-strategy runners. Clear of Maini, Tsolov pulled two seconds in a handful of laps and re-established the buffer his strategy needed.

The last five laps: fresh softs against old hards

The alternative-strategy leaders pitted between Laps 23 and 26 — Minì first of them, locking up his cold softs on the out-lap, then Villagómez after 24 laps on the hard tyre, then León and finally Goethe. With three laps to go Tsolov inherited the lead outright, but the race was not formally over: Villagómez, on softs only a few laps old, was taking chunks out of the gap corner by corner.

It never got close enough. Tsolov had managed his hards precisely for this phase, crossed the line 3.233 seconds clear, and only admitted afterwards that the car had developed a gearbox problem on the final lap — half-second delays on upshifts, one moment where it dropped into neutral. Villagómez took a career-best second, Maini held third, and Minì recovered from tenth on the grid to sixth — good damage limitation on a bad weekend, and nowhere near enough.

What the sweep actually means

Double-win weekends are rare in F2 by design. The reverse-grid Sprint exists precisely to stop the fastest qualifier winning everything, which is why nobody had done it for two and a half seasons. Doing it from P5 in qualifying — winning the Sprint from sixth on the grid and the Feature from fifth — is the harder version of an already rare result.

The championship arithmetic is now a different sport from a week ago. Tsolov leads by 17 with seven rounds left, holds six wins to Minì's one, and has out-scored the Italian 79 to 44 across the last four rounds. For the Super Licence maths, the question is effectively closed: he needs P5 or better in the final standings to clear 40 points, and he leads the championship. What changes for Spa

The Honest Verdict

He is no longer chasing the championship. He is the championship.

Since Montréal, every round has gone the same way: Tsolov scores heavily, his rivals take turns having problems. Monaco broke Câmara's season, Barcelona and Spielberg wore Minì down, and Silverstone — supposedly a weak circuit for Campos — produced the most complete weekend any F2 driver has delivered since 2024.

Spa is two weeks away, a power circuit with weather roulette and a slipstream-heavy racing pattern that tends to compress fields and randomise Sprints. A 17-point lead is not safe in a format that can hand out 39 points a weekend. But the pattern of this season is no longer about circuits. Tsolov has now won at a street track, a power track, a high-degradation track, and the fastest sequence of corners in racing — from pole, from P3, from P5, from P6. There is no circuit type left on the calendar where he has not already proven the argument.

Minì needs the problem weekends to start happening to the other car. Seven rounds remain.

Frequently asked questions

Who won the F2 Silverstone 2026 Feature Race?

Nikola Tsolov (Campos Racing) won the Silverstone Feature Race on 5 July 2026 by 3.233 seconds over Rafael Villagómez (Van Amersfoort Racing), with Kush Maini (ART Grand Prix) third. It completed a double-win weekend after his Sprint Race victory on Saturday.

When was the last F2 double-win weekend before this one?

Zane Maloney swept both races in Bahrain for Rodin at the start of the 2024 season. Nobody had repeated it in roughly two and a half seasons of racing until Tsolov's Silverstone weekend on 4–5 July 2026.

How did Tsolov pass Kush Maini for the lead of the pit-stop group?

After two attempts around the outside of Turn 9, Tsolov went around the outside of Maini on the entry to the Maggotts–Becketts complex — a sequence taken at very high speed where overtaking is almost never attempted because there is only one racing line. The pass gave him the clean air he needed before the alternative-strategy cars made their stops.

What tyre strategy did Tsolov use in the Feature Race?

The standard route: start on the soft, stop early. He pitted on Lap 6 along with the other soft-tyre front-runners, then ran 23 laps on one set of hards and managed them so he still had pace when the long-strategy runners — led by Villagómez — came out on fresh softs for the final five laps. The gap at the flag was 3.233 seconds.

What is the F2 championship situation after Silverstone 2026?

Tsolov leads on 141 points from Minì on 124 — a gap of 17. Câmara and Dunne follow on 92 each, with Câmara third on wins countback, and León is fifth on 69. Tsolov has six wins from seven rounds. The season continues at Spa-Francorchamps on 17–19 July, with seven rounds remaining.
